Exclusive experiences

Exclusive experiences only curation can deliver

From a private dinner to a closed-door winery: what changes when a trip has real curation behind it.

Anyone can book a flight and a hotel on their own. What a travel curation adds is precisely what's not available on any booking platform — access, relationships, and destination knowledge. A few examples of what this means in practice:

Wine tourism with access to closed-door wineries

On itineraries through Alentejo or France, ATF Travel's curation in Gastronomy and Sommellerie opens the doors of wineries that normally don't receive visitors without a referral, with guided tastings and pairings designed for the group.

Dinners and in-demand reservations

In destinations like Courchevel, getting a table at a Michelin-starred restaurant during peak season usually takes months of planning — or a network of relationships that solves it in days.

Invisible logistics at snow destinations

At resorts like Vail or Aspen, the difference lies in things the traveler doesn't even notice: rental equipment already waiting in the right size, a private ski lesson booked well in advance, lodging just steps from the lift.

Private tours instead of group ones

At all-inclusive resorts like those in Jamaica, or in regions like Xcaret, swapping a generic group tour for a private experience completely changes the pace of the trip — and usually requires knowing the right supplier, not just booking the most visible package.
